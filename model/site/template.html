PMRF: a personalized, multimodal model of post-meal glucose

From one meal photo, the last two hours of CGM and heart rate, and what it learned about the person in their first week, the model predicts the next two hours of glucose with a calibrated range, explains why, suggests diet changes for that person, and estimates their metabolic risk.

Status {{DATE}}: trained and tested on public data with the challenge protocol (learn days 1–7, predict meals from day 8 on, from the photo only). The official challenge data is not released yet; the pipeline is ready to run on it.

Personalization happens in three places: a profile of the person built from days 1–7 (average rise, rise per 10 g of carbs, glucose level and variability, resting heart rate), retrieval of the person's own past meals that look like the new one together with how they responded, and the transformer, which reads those past meal–curve pairs directly as context. None of these need retraining per person, so a new user is ready as soon as their first week is uploaded.

Beyond the curve: diet guidance and metabolic risk

Personal diet guide. Each person's carbohydrate sensitivity and the effect of adding protein or fat, measured in their own first-week standardized meals, give a carb budget per meal (rise of 50 mg/dL or less), their biggest spikes and the change that helps them most. {{GUIDE_SUMMARY}}
Chance of a high peak. Every prediction includes the probability of going above 140 and 180 mg/dL. {{EXCEED_SUMMARY}}

Why personalization

08

Limits

  • Small data. 45 test participants over about 10 days; one CGM brand.
  • Photos. Estimating carbohydrate from a single photo is still coarse for home-cooked meals.
  • Unseen factors. Activity after the meal, sleep and stress are not inputs; about a quarter of the variation is day-to-day noise no model can predict.
  • Range. The 80% band covers {{BAND_COV}}% on new days, slightly narrow; the app should widen it by 10%.
  • Medication. Validated on insulin in type 1 diabetes only; oral drugs will be learned from the challenge data.
  • Risk scores. Built on small, different populations (Spanish hypertension clinic, Shanghai hospital); treat as research estimates.
09

Vision-language model pilot (Qwen2.5-VL-3B)

Can a general vision-language model read nutrition from photos better than our photo model? We ran Qwen2.5-VL-3B on every meal photo on the CPU ({{VLM_SEC}} s per photo).

  • Accuracy. Zero-shot, the 3B model underestimates carbohydrate by a median of {{VLM_BIAS}} g and is less accurate than our estimator, most of all on the standardized breakfasts and lunches, which our estimator recognises through photo retrieval. On free-living meals, averaging the two estimates is the most accurate, so the two sources are complementary.
  • What it adds. Readable dish descriptions ("Caesar salad with chicken and bacon", "slice of carrot cake with cream cheese frosting") that the app could show next to the prediction.

Deployment plan (AWS)

The deployment package is built and tested: one CPU-only Docker image (0.86 GB) that runs the endpoints above. In a container limited to 2 vCPU and 4 GB it starts in 3 seconds, uses about 1 GB of memory and answers a prediction, what-ifs included, in 252 ms (median; 374 ms at the 99th percentile), about 3.8 predictions per second per container. For engineers. Everything below is also in README.md and the code under pmrf/ and scripts/.

Data, labels and protocol

  • Development data. CGMacros (PhysioNet v1.0.0): 45 adults (15 healthy, 16 prediabetes, 14 T2D by HbA1c), about 10 days each, Dexcom G6 Pro CGM, Fitbit heart rate, 1,706 logged meals with nutrition and 1,644 usable meal photos. Dexcom is used throughout because, like the challenge CGM, it samples every 5 minutes; the Libre stream reads 32 mg/dL lower on average and is not used (see the EDA report).
  • Pretraining and medication data. The harmonized T1D file: isolated carbohydrate entries of at least 10 g with a contiguous 5-minute CGM window, from the 9 studies that log carbs ({{T1D_EVENTS}} events, {{T1D_SUBJECTS}} people), with bolus, basal and insulin-on-board computed around each meal.
  • Labels. From the CGM interpolated between native readings: glucose at 30/60/90/120 min, maximum in 0–120 min, Δpeak, time to peak, iAUC 0–120 min. A meal is labelled when CGM exists at the meal time, at least 90% of −30…120 min is covered, and no other meal starts within 120 minutes after it. {{N_LABELLED}} of 1,706 meals qualify.
  • Protocol. Mirrors the challenge: days 1–7 of every participant are history (nutrition labels available), meals from day 8 on are predicted from the photo only. All tuning uses days 1–5 → 6–7; the day-8+ test set is scored once.

Uncertainty details

  • Ranges. On day 8+, {{BAND_COV}}% of observed curve points fall inside the nominal 80% band ({{BAND_COV60}}% at 60 min, {{BAND_COV120}}% at 120 min), so the band is slightly too narrow on later days. Its width was set on days 6–7 (79.5% there) and deliberately not re-tuned. On the test days a band 10% wider covers 80.4%; we propose that as the app default and flag it as the one setting informed by the test set.
  • Knowing when it does not know. The confidence level drops when the history is short, the meal type never appeared in days 1–7, the photo looks unlike anything seen, or CGM is missing before the meal. On the test days, meals with any flag have a larger Δpeak error ({{FLAG_ANY}} vs {{FLAG_NONE}} mg/dL, Table 4); the photo-novelty flag fired only {{FLAG_PHOTO_N}} times and is not informative yet.
  • Limits. 45 people over about 10 days is small; free-living carbohydrate estimation from one photo is coarse (median error about 55%); the cohort has no medication data, so the medication module is validated on T1D insulin only; post-meal activity, which the model cannot see, adds noise; results come from one CGM brand (Dexcom G6).
  • Clinical and consumer use. Pre-meal coaching ("this plate will likely take you above 180 mg/dL; half the rice keeps you in range"), clinician review of which meals drive excursions for a patient, and a starting point for titrating meal-time medication, always as decision support alongside clinical judgment.

Metabolic profile and risk models

HbA1c: ridge regression on 7-day CGM metrics, trained on outpatient cohorts only (pooling in the Shanghai hospital cohort biased outpatient estimates by about +1 point); 10×5-fold CV; leave-one-cohort-out reported in results/risk/summary.json. Future T2D: L2 logistic regression, 20×5-fold stratified CV, at most 4 CGM predictors (17 events). Complications: cross-sectional association only. Diet guide: personal shake contrasts (days 1–7) shrunk toward the group with weight 1 serving; validated on 94 day-8+ shakes.
